About This Course

+

College Physics is algebra-based physics, meaning it uses algebra and trigonometry rather than calculus. In many U.S. high schools it's taken in the junior or senior year, though it's designed to work as a first-year college course. Before starting, you should be comfortable with trigonometry, basic geometry, and algebra, including quadratic equations.

Algebra-Based vs. Calculus-Based Physics

This course, and this video library, cover algebra-based physics. There's also a calculus-based version, often called University Physics or AP Physics C, typically required for physics, engineering, and similar majors. Algebra-based physics is a common choice for life sciences, pre-health fields, or a general science requirement. If you're not sure which one your intended major needs, check with an advisor before choosing.

Broad Topics in Physics

  • Mechanics: motion, forces, energy, momentum, rotational motion, and equilibrium
  • Fluids: fluid statics and fluid dynamics
  • Thermodynamics: heat, temperature, and energy transfer
  • Electricity and Magnetism: electric charge, circuits, magnetic fields, and induction
  • Waves and Optics: sound, light, and wave behavior
  • Modern Physics: an introduction to relativity and quantum concepts
This video library currently covers Mechanics and Fluids (Chapters 1 through 12), which lines up closely with the AP Physics 1 curriculum. The remaining topics may be added later.
